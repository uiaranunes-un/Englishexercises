B1 — Past Simple & Past Continuous

Talking about finished past events and actions that were in progress in the past.

Reference Box

1. Past Simple

Use the Past Simple for actions that started and finished in the past.

Subject Verb Example
I / You / He / She / We / They past form I worked yesterday.
She went to the supermarket.

Regular verbs usually end in -ed:

work → worked   |   watch → watched   |   play → played   |   study → studied

Negative

Use didn't + base verb.

I didn't work yesterday.
She didn't go to work.
After didn't, use the base form:
didn't go ✅    didn't went ❌

Questions

Use Did + subject + base verb?

Did you work yesterday?
Did she go to the supermarket?
Did she go...? ✅    Did she went...? ❌

2. Past Continuous

Use the Past Continuous for an action that was in progress at a particular moment in the past.

Form: was / were + verb-ing

Subject Form Example
I / He / She / It was + -ing She was working.
You / We / They were + -ing They were eating.
At 8:00 last night, I was watching TV.
They were having dinner.

Negative: I wasn't sleeping. / They weren't working.

Questions: Were you sleeping? / Was he working?

3. Past Simple + Past Continuous

We often use the two tenses together. The Past Continuous describes an action already in progress. The Past Simple describes another event that happened during that action.

I was cooking when the phone rang.
We were walking home when it started to rain.

With while, we often describe an action in progress:

While I was studying, my brother was watching TV.
