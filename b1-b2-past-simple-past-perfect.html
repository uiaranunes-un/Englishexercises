Past simple and past perfect

Talking about past events and the order in which they happened

Reference box

TenseFormExample
Past simpleverb + -ed / irregular formWe arrived at eight. / She left early.
Past simple — negativedidn’t + base formI didn’t see him.
Past simple — questionDid + subject + base form?Did you call her?
Past perfecthad + past participleThe film had started.
Past perfect — negativehadn’t + past participleThey hadn’t met before.
Past perfect — questionHad + subject + past participle?Had you visited the city before?

Use the past simple for completed past events. Use the past perfect to look back at an earlier event from a point in the past.

When we arrived, the film had started.
→ The film started first. We arrived later.

When we arrived, the film started.
→ We arrived first. Then the film started.

The past perfect is useful when the earlier event matters to the story. You don’t need it for every event that happened earlier, especially when words such as before and after already make the order clear.

3. Write one sentence

Use the beginning given and the past perfect for the earlier event. Write the complete sentence.
Example: I finished my homework. Then my friend called.
By the time my friend called, I had finished my homework.

Other sentences may also be correct. If your wording is different, compare it with the model answer and ask your teacher.

Use the past simple or past perfect.

Last Friday, Nina (1) ___ (go) to the airport to fly to Madrid. She (2) ___ (feel) excited because she (3) ___ (never / travel) abroad before.
